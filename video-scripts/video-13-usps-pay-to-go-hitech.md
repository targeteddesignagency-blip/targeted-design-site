### THE PLAY (0:15-0:40)

**[VISUAL: USPS promo document animates open. Approved technologies slide out like a menu:]**

**NARRATOR:**
For campaigns running up to six consecutive months in 2026, the USPS is offering a 5 percent discount on postage — but only if your mail piece incorporates approved digital technologies.

**[VISUAL: Five tech icons appear in a row, each with a brief demo clip:]**

They're talking about:

→ **Virtual Reality** — cardboard pop-up lenses in the mailer that let you preview a beach vacation in 360 degrees.

→ **Augmented Reality** — point your phone at the postcard and a 3D model of the product appears on your living room table.

→ **NFC chips** — the same tech in your contactless credit card, embedded in the paper. Tap your phone to the mailer and it instantly launches a video.

→ **Video in Print** — actual thin-screen LCD displays built into catalogs that play HD commercials right there in your hands.

→ **Voice triggers** — directional copy that tells you to ask your smart speaker to take you somewhere, creating attributed voice search traffic.

### THE ARBITRAGE (0:40-1:05)

**[VISUAL: Two columns — "Expensive Route" vs. "Smart Route"]**

**NARRATOR:**
Now, video-in-print screens are expensive. We're talking 50 bucks per unit. That's reserved for high-ticket B2B or luxury real estate — where one client pays for the whole campaign.

**[VISUAL: "Expensive Route" column fades. "Smart Route" column expands.]**

But here's the play. Marketers game the system with cheap tech that still qualifies.

**NARRATOR:**
Print a dynamic QR code that launches an AI-powered chatbot.

**[VISUAL: QR code on a postcard. Phone scans it. A clean chatbot conversation appears: "Hi! Interested in a free HVAC inspection? Tap YES."]**

Production cost? Virtually nothing. But that QR code still qualifies for the 5 percent postage discount across millions of units.

**[VISUAL: Calculator animation: postage savings stack up. "5% × 1,000,000 units = MASSIVE"]**

That's a brilliant arbitrage. The marketer gets cheaper postage. The USPS forces the adoption of interactive mail. Both sides win.

### THE REAL CHALLENGE: ATTRIBUTION (1:05-1:40)

**[VISUAL: A CFO character staring at a spreadsheet, confused. "Did the mail actually work?"]**

**NARRATOR:**
But here's the real question. If you send out a million AI-targeted, NFC-enabled postcards — how do you prove to your finance team that the mail actually drove revenue?

**[VISUAL: Answer builds piece by piece]**

**First — avoid the single method trap.** One promo code on a card? You're blinding yourself. You need at least three tracking bridges.

**[VISUAL: Three bridges appear as literal bridges connecting "Physical Mail" island to "Digital Dashboard" island:]**

→ **QR codes and personalized URLs** for the digital-first crowd
→ **Unique call tracking numbers** for the phone-callers
→ **Matchback analysis** after the campaign — comparing your mailing list against purchases to find the silent converters who never clicked anything

**Second — fight last-touch bias.** Your Google Analytics dashboard will give Google credit for searches that your mailer actually triggered. If you only trust last-touch data, you'll cut your mail budget and wonder why organic search dies.

**[VISUAL: Org chart showing the real chain: Postcard → Memory → Google Search → Purchase. Highlight: "The mail started this."]**

**Third — run incrementality tests.** Hold out 10 to 20 percent as a control group. Compare conversion rates. The difference is your proven, mathematical, no-doubt-about-it return on mail.

**[VISUAL: Email notification on phone: "You've got mail!" with grayscale envelope preview. Then a full-color digital ad slides in beside it.]**

And there's one more weapon most people sleep on: Informed Delivery.

**[VISUAL: Counter ticking up: 60M... 61M... 62M households]**

The USPS currently sends daily emails to over 62 million American households, showing them grayscale scans of their incoming mail.

Marketers attach full-color, clickable digital ads to those scans.

You're converting on mail that hasn't been delivered yet.
